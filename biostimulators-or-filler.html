Biostimulators or filler

Sculptra and Radiesse against hyaluronic acid. They are not really rivals. One builds the foundation and the other defines the edges, and most plans want both, in that order.

Written by Dr. Shankar Mundluru, MD MBA
Board-certified physician · Founder, Aahana Medical Aesthetics
San Francisco · Dogpatch
Reviewed September 2026

Filler is the material. Hyaluronic acid gel is placed where volume is wanted, and it is the gel you are seeing. It works the day it goes in, and it can be dissolved with an enzyme if you do not like it.

Biostimulators are not the material. Poly-L-lactic acid in Sculptra and calcium hydroxylapatite in Radiesse provoke your own fibroblasts to lay down collagen, and the particles themselves break down and leave. What you end up seeing is tissue you made. That takes months, and it cannot be dissolved, because by the time you can see it there is nothing foreign left to dissolve.

Everything else follows from that one difference.

01

The trade-off, stated plainly

Filler buysControl

You see the result in the chair. It can be adjusted, and it can be removed. The right answer for a specific shape in a specific place.

Biostimulators buyLongevity

Your own collagen arrives gradually across an area, so the face reads as improved rather than added to. The cost: patience, and no undo. If you dislike it, you wait it out.

Anyone who says one is simply better is describing their own preference, not the treatments.

02

Why it cannot be judged at two weeks

  1. Right afterYou look fuller. Sculptra is suspended in liquid, and that is the liquid.
  2. Within daysThe liquid absorbs and you look as you did before. Expected, not a failure.
  3. From week 4The collagen response you are waiting for begins to arrive.

People who are not told this often decide at two weeks that nothing worked.

Which suits which problem

Spread-out lossBiostimulators

Diffuse volume loss across the midface, temples or whole upper face. Also hands and chest, where Radiesse is used and contour filler would look wrong.

A specific shapeFiller

Chin projection, a defined jawline edge, lip shape. These need a material that stays where it is placed. Collagen stimulation does not substitute.

After rapid weight loss

Volume lost during GLP-1 treatment is diffuse, which points toward biostimulators. But wait until your weight has stabilized before treating a face that is still changing.

03

What we recommend

Both, in that order. They are complementary, not competing.

  1. Biostimulator courseRebuilds the broad foundation. A supported face usually needs less filler at the end, not more.
  2. Read from week 4See what the collagen has done before the filler conversation.
  3. Filler for the edgesWhatever still needs definition. Doing it first means judging filler against a face about to change underneath it.
Never the same appointment

The aftercare instructions contradict each other, one result is immediate and the other takes months, and stacking them makes it impossible to tell which did what.

Common questions

If biostimulators cannot be dissolved, what happens if I do not like the result?

You wait, and it fades over a year or two as that collagen turns over like any other. There is no enzyme for it, because what you are seeing is your own tissue rather than an implanted material. This is the strongest argument for starting conservatively and building across sessions rather than trying to reach the endpoint in one visit, and it is why the treatment plan is deliberately incremental.

Is one better value?

Per vial, filler is usually cheaper. Per year of result, biostimulators often come out ahead, because they last longer and a completed series holds for one to two years or more. But a series is several appointments and the cost arrives up front, so the cash flow is different even where the arithmetic favors it. Both are discussed at consultation rather than quoted from a list.

Does skin type affect which one I can have?

Neither is restricted by Fitzpatrick type, because neither works by delivering heat or light into the skin. What does matter in richly pigmented skin is any history of keloid or hypertrophic scarring, and that is asked about before either treatment. Injection technique and the number of entry points are adjusted accordingly.

What is the massage rule and does it actually matter?

With Sculptra the convention is five minutes, five times a day, for five days, and yes it matters. It distributes the product evenly through the tissue and reduces the chance of nodules forming where it has clumped. Filler is the opposite instruction: do not touch it, do not press it, do not massage it. Getting these two backwards is a real risk if you have had both, which is why the aftercare is given separately each time.

Can I have both in the same appointment?

It is not the usual plan. The aftercare instructions contradict each other, one result appears immediately and the other over months, and assessing what the filler achieved is harder when collagen is building underneath it at the same time. Sequencing them, with biostimulators first, gives a cleaner read on what each one actually did.

Where does skin quality fit into this?

Biostimulators do improve skin quality as a by-product of building collagen, which is part of why people like them. But if quality is the main complaint rather than volume, there are treatments aimed directly at it: SkinVive for smoothness on the cheeks and neck, PRP for texture and tone, microneedling for surface texture. Using a volume treatment to chase a surface problem is how faces end up overfilled.
