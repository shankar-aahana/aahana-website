Topical exosomes

Applied to the skin after microneedling, as an addition to that treatment. An emerging area, with published clinical work behind it.

Exosomes are extracellular vesicles, the small packets cells release to carry proteins and genetic material to other cells. In aesthetics they are used as a topical preparation applied to skin immediately after microneedling, while the channels created by the needling are still open.

Here they are used topically and only after microneedling, as an addition to that treatment. Nothing is injected.

What the published work reports

A 2026 systematic review in the Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology gathered the clinical studies of microneedling with exosomes. It found eight studies covering 171 patients, screened from 256 references.

Across those studies, investigators reported increases in hair density in androgenetic alopecia, wrinkle reduction in the range of 12 to 14 percent, and improvement in pigmentation measures, with objective dark spot coverage falling from 40 percent to 27 percent in the studies that measured it. No patient in any of the studies discontinued treatment because of an adverse effect. Redness, swelling and small areas of bruising occurred and resolved within days to a week.

The shape of that work

Study sizeEight studies and 171 patients in total, mostly case series and split-face pilots
Follow-upSix months is the longest any of them followed patients
ProductsExosome sources differed between studies, so results do not necessarily carry from one preparation to another
StandardsThere is no agreed definition of what a given product contains

The authors point to larger randomized trials as the next step.

Common questions

Can it be added to any microneedling appointment?

Yes, and it is decided at the appointment rather than in advance. It adds a few minutes and an additional fee, and it does not change the microneedling protocol, the settings used or the aftercare.

Do you inject exosomes?

No. Only topical application after microneedling. Injection is not offered here.

How much evidence is there?

Eight studies covering 171 patients, with follow-up out to six months. The reported results were positive across skin texture, pigmentation and hair density, and no patient in any of them stopped treatment because of a side effect. They are early studies rather than large randomized trials, and the authors point to larger trials as the next step.

Is it better than PRP?

They have not been compared head to head, so there is no answer from the evidence. PRP has a larger evidence base and uses your own blood rather than a manufactured product. Which suits you depends on what is being treated, and that is decided at consultation.

Who should not have this?

The exclusions are those for microneedling itself: active infection or inflammatory skin disease in the treatment area, certain wound-healing disorders, and pregnancy. Anyone with a history of significant allergic reaction should raise it before anything is applied.

How to prepare

Three days before

  • Stop over-the-counter anti-inflammatories. Ibuprofen, naproxen, fish oil, vitamin E and flaxseed oil. These reduce platelet activity and directly undermine the treatment. This is the most important instruction on this page.
  • Stop alcohol. It affects platelet function and increases bruising.
  • Stop high-dose vitamin C above 1,000 mg a day.
  • Do not stop prescribed blood thinners or anticoagulants such as aspirin taken for cardiac reasons, apixaban, rivaroxaban, warfarin or clopidogrel. Do not stop any of these without speaking to the doctor who prescribed them. Contact us instead and we will plan around them.

Tell us before your appointment

  • Any clotting disorder or platelet condition.
  • Any history of blood cancer, or any active cancer. PRP is not performed in either case.
  • Any active infection, rash or open wound in the treatment area.
  • Your current medications, particularly corticosteroids and immunosuppressants, which affect platelet function and healing.
  • For scalp treatment, any scalp condition such as psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis or a fungal infection. These are treated first.
  • If you are pregnant or breastfeeding.
  • Any known allergies.

The day itself

  • Drink water and eat something. Hydration measurably affects the quality of the plasma drawn.
  • Arrive with the treatment area clean and no products on it.
  • For scalp treatment, wash your hair that morning with a gentle shampoo and use no styling product.
  • Wear something comfortable. A button-front top is easier for facial treatment.

The first 24 hours

  • No anti-inflammatories for three days. No ibuprofen, no naproxen. The inflammatory response after treatment is part of how PRP works, and suppressing it reduces the result. If you need something for discomfort, ask us what to take instead.
  • No ice directly on the skin. It constricts vessels and dampens the same response. A gentle cool compress is fine if swelling is marked.
  • Swelling, redness and small bumps at the injection sites are normal, particularly under the eye and on the scalp.
  • Do not touch, rub or massage the injection sites.
  • No vigorous exercise for 24 hours.
  • Avoid heat: saunas, steam rooms and very hot showers.
  • No alcohol for 24 hours.
  • Gentle skincare is fine on the face from the evening: cleanser and a fragrance-free moisturizer. SPF 50 the next morning.

When to contact us straight away

  • Any change in vision after facial or under-eye treatment. Seek emergency care immediately.
  • Swelling that is worsening after 48 hours rather than settling.
  • Signs of infection at any injection site: spreading redness, warmth, pus or fever.
  • Numbness or tingling that lasts beyond 24 hours.
  • On the scalp, significant pain or swelling, or any sign of infection.
