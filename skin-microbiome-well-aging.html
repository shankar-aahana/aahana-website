Your skin microbiome is already producing compounds that support well aging

One of your resident bacteria secretes an antioxidant protein. Another builds the ceramides in your barrier. This is not a system waiting to be fixed.

By Dr. Shankar Mundluru, MD MBA
Board-certified physician · Founder, Aahana Medical Aesthetics
September 5, 2026 · San Francisco

The short version

  • Cutibacterium acnes secretes RoxP, a protein with genuine antioxidant activity, onto your skin.
  • Staphylococcus epidermidis produces ceramides, the lipids that hold your barrier together.
  • Which means the reflex to sterilize skin is often working against you.
  • Baseline microbiomes differ by population, so generic microbiome advice frequently does not transfer.
  • Testing is genuinely informative and is not a diagnostic test. The distinction matters.

The skincare industry has spent a long time selling the idea that bacteria on your face are the enemy. Antibacterial cleansers, sterilizing toners, the whole vocabulary of purifying and detoxifying.

The biology says something close to the opposite. Some of the most useful chemistry happening on your skin is not being produced by your skin. It is being produced by the organisms living on it, and they were doing it before you bought anything.

Your bacteria make an antioxidant

Start with the most striking example.

  1. RoxPCutibacterium acnes, the organism with the worst reputation on human skin, secretes radical oxygenase, a protein with strong antioxidant activity.
  2. It protectsShields keratinocytes and monocytes from oxidative damage, measurably improving cell viability under oxidative stress.1
  3. Its absence tracks diseaseIn oxidative skin disease RoxP declines in step: lower in actinic keratosis than in the same patient's healthy skin.1

Oxidative stress is central to skin aging, and the whole antioxidant category of skincare exists to address it.

The organism with the worst reputation on human skin has been making you an antioxidant this entire time.

And they build your barrier

The second example is structural rather than chemical.

CeramidesHold the barrier together

The lipids binding the outermost layer and keeping water in; why barrier creams list them on the box. Your skin makes its own, but not the whole supply.

Staphylococcus epidermidisMakes some for you

This commensal generates ceramides that contribute directly to barrier homeostasis.2

Not a population to suppress. A working part of the organ.

Which is why sterilizing your face backfires

Patients arrive constantly with stripped skin:

Foaming cleansers twice dailyAlcohol tonersBenzoyl peroxide as a face washAntibacterial soap

Tight, reactive, and paradoxically breaking out more than when they started. A reasonable reading: months spent reducing the population that makes their antioxidant and part of their barrier lipid. Sensitivity is what a failing barrier looks like.

Not an argument against treating acne

Acne is real and needs real treatment. The argument is against treating bacteria as the target, when the issue is which organisms are present and in what balance.

Your baseline is not universal

The part most microbiome content skips, and why I am careful about generic advice.

The study

200 samples from Chinese individuals compared against datasets from the United States, Tanzania and mainland China found race-based clustering in community membership and structure, with differing abundances of Propionibacterium, Corynebacterium, Staphylococcus and Enhydrobacter.3 The authors: microbiome-altering interventions may not be effective across population groups.3

Measuring your own baseline beats inheriting somebody else's.

A protocol built on one population's normal can aim at the wrong target for another: the problem in device selection and skin typing, one layer down.

What a swab actually gives you

Sequencing a swab describes what is growing on you: which organisms, in what proportion, plus diversity and a microbiome type.4 More than a category, less than a diagnosis. Composition shifts with body site, season, products and that morning's wash, so one result is a reading, and the pattern shows when you measure again after a change. Links to outcomes are still mostly associative, so it belongs in a plan, not as the plan.

Removing one strain without removing the rest

Here the argument becomes a treatment. If your resident organisms make an antioxidant and part of your barrier lipid, anything that clears bacteria broadly costs you something. Antibiotics and sterilizing routines do not distinguish: the strain driving inflammation and the strain making RoxP go together.

Broad-spectrum antibioticActs across many species

The helpful strains go with the harmful ones.

BacteriophageRecognizes one strain

A virus that infects bacteria, specific to particular strains, killing by lysis. Less collateral damage to the rest of the microbiome.5

An antibiotic clears the room. A phage removes one guest.

The early data. In mice with C. acnes acne-like lesions, a topical phage gel reduced lesion size, elevation and bacterial load, and sharply cut neutrophil infiltration and inflammatory signals including IL-6, TNF-alpha and IL-17. The phages penetrated intact skin and stayed viable at the lesion.6

Safety. Phages target bacteria, not human cells. They are among the most abundant biological entities on earth and already live on your skin. Across clinical studies, serious adverse events have been extremely rare, and reported effects mild and transient.7

I spent years working with this technology, and what convinced me was watching skin that had not responded to the usual treatments improve on a preparation built for what was actually growing on it. The published human literature is still building, much of it animal and in vitro work.5 That is about how much has been formally trialled, not about whether it is safe to use.

Common questions

What does the skin microbiome actually do?

It contributes directly to skin function rather than just living there. Cutibacterium acnes secretes RoxP, a protein with antioxidant activity that protects skin cells from oxidative damage. Commensal Staphylococcus epidermidis generates ceramides that contribute to barrier homeostasis. Resident organisms also help maintain surface pH and compete with pathogens.

Is Cutibacterium acnes bad for skin?

Not inherently. It is one of the dominant organisms on healthy human skin and secretes RoxP, an antioxidant protein that improves skin cell survival under oxidative stress. Levels of RoxP decline in skin affected by oxidative disease. Acne involves the balance and behavior of specific strains rather than simply the presence of the species.

Can over-cleansing damage your skin microbiome?

Aggressive routines that strip the skin reduce the resident organisms that produce barrier lipids and antioxidant compounds, and the result often presents as sensitivity, tightness and paradoxically more breakouts. Treating acne is legitimate; treating all skin bacteria as the enemy is a different thing and frequently counterproductive.

Is skin microbiome testing worth it?

It earns its place when the obvious answer has already failed: persistent inflammatory conditions that have not responded to reasonable treatment, recurrent acne with a pattern that does not fit, or a barrier damaged by an aggressive routine. It shows which organisms are actually present rather than what a category assumes. Composition shifts with site, season and recent products, so a single result is a reading rather than a verdict, and it belongs in a plan rather than being the plan.

What is phage therapy for skin?

Bacteriophages are viruses that infect bacteria, and they are extraordinarily specific, recognizing particular strains rather than whole species. That specificity is the point: unlike a broad-spectrum antibiotic, a phage can remove the strains driving a problem while leaving the organisms producing antioxidants and barrier lipids intact. In a mouse model of C. acnes induced acne-like lesions, topical phage reduced lesion size, bacterial load and inflammatory signaling. Phages target bacteria rather than human cells, and across clinical studies serious adverse events have been extremely rare. The published human efficacy literature is still building.

Does the skin microbiome differ by ethnicity?

Yes, measurably. Comparisons across populations show race-based clustering in both microbial community membership and structure, with differing abundances of common genera. The researchers concluded that microbiome-altering interventions may not be equally effective across population groups, which is a strong argument for measuring an individual baseline rather than applying generic advice.

Is skin microbiome testing the same as a skin DNA test?

No, and they are easy to confuse because both are DNA sequencing. A genetic skin test reads your own genome. It never changes, it is read once, and it tells you about tendency rather than anything you can act on directly. A microbiome swab reads the genes of the organisms living on your skin, which shift with season, products and what you washed with that morning. That is the point of it: it is a current measurement, it responds to what you change, and measuring again is how you find out whether the change worked.
