Hydrafacial

Cleansing, extraction and hydration in one session, with no recovery. Worth having on its own, and the reason every laser and peel series here begins with one.

A Hydrafacial runs four steps through a single device: a gentle exfoliating pass, vacuum extraction of the pores, an infusion of hyaluronic acid and antioxidants, and a targeted booster chosen for your skin. It is comfortable, it takes under an hour, and you leave looking better than when you arrived.

What it is not for

A Hydrafacial works at the surface. It is not the answer for problems that sit deeper than that.

  • Significant hyperpigmentation or melasma needs treatment aimed at melanin itself.
  • Scarring and textural change need something that reaches the dermis.
  • Volume loss and laxity are structural and need a different category of treatment altogether.

It is a foundation, and it is often the first step in a plan rather than the whole plan. What that plan looks like is decided at consultation.

Why every series starts with one

Before a laser, peel or microneedling series begins here, the first appointment is a Hydrafacial. Congestion is cleared, the skin is hydrated, and the barrier is put in the best condition it can be in before anything more significant is introduced.

This matters most in deeper skin. The risk with laser and peels in Fitzpatrick IV to VI is post-inflammatory pigmentation, and the level of inflammation the skin starts from is one of the things that decides whether you get it. A Hydrafacial improves hydration and barrier function without adding any meaningful inflammatory stimulus, which is exactly what you want in the weeks before.

The visit does double duty. It is also where skin condition is assessed, Visia findings are confirmed, and the plan is finalized with Dr. Mundluru.

Common questions

Is there any downtime?

No. This is the treatment you can have before an event rather than instead of one. Some people notice mild pinkness around areas that were extracted, and it settles within a few hours. Skin looks brighter and clearer the same day, and keeps improving over the next 24 to 48 hours.

Does it hurt?

No, and no numbing is needed. The exfoliating step is a mild tingle or nothing at all. Extraction is the step people notice, a pulling sensation that is strongest around the nose, and most people rather enjoy it. The hydration step is just pressure.

Why do I need one before starting laser or peels?

Because the condition your skin starts in changes the outcome. Clearing congestion and restoring hydration and barrier function before a series means the first real session lands on calm, healthy skin. In Fitzpatrick IV to VI that matters more, since the risk with laser and peels is post-inflammatory pigmentation and the starting level of inflammation is part of what drives it. A Hydrafacial improves the skin without adding inflammation of its own.

I broke out a few days afterwards. Is that normal?

Usually yes. Small breakouts in the days after extraction are a purge, congestion that was already under the surface coming up. It is temporary and clears on its own within about a week. If it looks out of proportion, or is still getting worse after 48 hours, contact us rather than waiting it out.

What do I need to stop beforehand?

Very little. Stop retinoids three to five days before, and stop high-strength exfoliating acids in the same window. Gentle toners are fine. Arrive with a clean face and no makeup. That is the whole list.

Will it help my pigmentation?

Not on its own. A Hydrafacial works at the surface and is not a treatment for significant hyperpigmentation or melasma. A brightening booster can be added at the hydration step, which delivers agents such as tranexamic acid, kojic acid or niacinamide into freshly cleared pores where they absorb well, and that is a useful contribution. But pigmentation is treated with something aimed at melanin, and the plan for that is set at consultation.

Can I have one if I am pregnant?

Tell us beforehand. Some of the boosters are not recommended during pregnancy, and the protocol is adjusted rather than the treatment being ruled out. The same applies during an active rosacea flare, where settings are changed and a calming booster is used instead of the standard one.

Is anyone not suitable for it?

A Hydrafacial cannot be performed over broken skin or significant inflammation, so active inflammatory acne, open wounds, rashes and skin infections in the treatment area need to settle or be treated first. Tell us about any laser, peel or microneedling in the last two weeks, and about any allergy or sensitivity, particularly to salicylic acid, which is used in the clarifying step.

How to prepare

There is genuinely very little to do for this one.

Three to five days before

  • Stop retinoids. Tretinoin, retinol, retinaldehyde. They raise sensitivity and make the treatment more irritating than it needs to be.
  • Stop high-strength exfoliating acids. Strong AHAs and BHAs. Gentle toners are fine to continue.

Tell us before your appointment

  • Any active inflammatory acne, open wound, rash or skin infection on the area. A Hydrafacial cannot be performed over broken skin or significant inflammation.
  • Any laser, peel or microneedling in the last two weeks.
  • Any allergy or sensitivity, particularly to salicylic acid, which is used in the clarifying step.
  • If you are pregnant. Some boosters are not used during pregnancy and the protocol is adjusted.
  • Any active rosacea flare. Settings are adjusted and a calming booster is used.

The day itself

  • Arrive with a clean face. No makeup, no heavy moisturizer, no SPF.
  • Nothing else.

The four steps

1. Cleanse and exfoliate

A vortex tip lifts surface dead cells and sebum, using a mild glycolic and salicylic blend that is far gentler than a standalone peel. Most people feel a light tingle or nothing.

2. Extract

Vacuum suction clears congestion, blackheads and debris from the pores. This is the step you feel, a pulling sensation strongest around the nose. It is not painful.

3. Hydrate

Hyaluronic acid, peptides and antioxidants are infused under light pressure into skin that has just been cleared, which is when it absorbs best.

4. Boost

A targeted booster chosen for your skin. Brightening where pigmentation is a concern, or calming peptides and growth factors for reactive skin and rosacea.

The rest of the day

Your pores have just been cleared. The aim for the rest of the day is to keep them that way.

  • No heavy makeup. Mineral makeup is fine if you need something.
  • No vigorous exercise. Sweat over freshly cleared pores is a reliable way to cause breakouts.
  • Avoid heat. Saunas, steam rooms and very hot showers.
  • SPF 50 if you are going outside, which applies always but particularly now.
  • This is the best window of the month for a good serum. Skin absorbs more right after treatment than at any other time.

The following week

  • Back to your normal routine from the next morning. Cleanser, moisturizer, SPF.
  • If you stopped retinoids before the appointment, restart them after three days.
  • SPF 50 every morning.
  • A mild purge is common. Small breakouts in the days after extraction are congestion coming to the surface. It is temporary and resolves on its own within about a week.
  • As maintenance, sessions every four to six weeks hold the result.

When to contact us

This treatment has essentially no serious complication profile. Contact us if:

  • A breakout or rash develops that seems out of proportion, or is still worsening after 48 hours.
  • You react to one of the boosters: itching, hives, or redness that is not settling.
  • Anything at all concerns you. There is no threshold too low here.
