Care instructions

Migraine and headache

Before and after Botox for migraine, and occipital and supraorbital nerve blocks.

For patients of Aahana Medical Aesthetics. If something does not match what you were told in person, what you were told in person is right. Call before you act on anything here.

Having more than one thing done? All care instructions has every treatment on one page.

Before your visit

There is no special preparation. A few things help us plan the treatment.

Bring

  • Your headache diary, or a rough count: headache days per month over the last three months, and how many of those were severe.
  • A list of your current medications, including what you take when a headache starts and anything you take to prevent them.
  • What you have already tried, and how well each thing worked.

Keep taking

  • Your usual headache and migraine medications, unless we have told you otherwise.
  • Prescribed blood thinners. Do not stop them. Tell us and we plan around them.

Tell us before

  • If you are pregnant or breastfeeding.
  • Any allergy to a numbing medication or to steroids.
  • If you have diabetes. A steroid in a nerve block can raise blood sugar for a day or two.
  • Any rash, cut or infection where the injection would go.
  • Previous Botox or nerve blocks for headache: when, and how long relief lasted.

The day itself

  • Eat beforehand.
  • Clean hair and skin, with no hair products at the back of the head or on the forehead.
  • Most people drive themselves home. For a first nerve block, have someone you can call if you feel lightheaded.

After Botox for migraine

The first four hours

  • Stay upright.
  • Do not rub or massage the injection sites. Forehead, head, neck and shoulders.
  • No hard exercise, sauna or hot tub for the rest of the day.

The first weeks

  • Mild headache, or neck and shoulder soreness, for one to two days is common.
  • Prevention builds over two to four weeks. Some people need two treatment cycles for the full benefit.
  • Keep a headache diary. It is how we judge the result and adjust the next session.
  • Keep taking your usual headache medications.
  • Repeats every twelve weeks.

After a nerve block

Occipital is the back of the head. Supraorbital is above the eyebrow. The instructions below apply to both.

The first day

  • Numbness over the treated area for a few hours is expected.
  • Relief can start within minutes. If a steroid was included, its longer effect builds over a few days, so a headache can briefly return when the numbness wears off.
  • Soreness or a small bruise for a day or two. A cold pack, ten minutes at a time, helps.
  • Take it easy for the rest of the day. Showering is fine.
  • If you have diabetes and a steroid was used, check your blood sugar more often for a day or two.

After that

  • Keep a headache diary, noting how long the relief lasts. That is what decides when, or whether, to repeat the block.
  • Keep taking your usual headache medications.

When to contact us straight away
  • Difficulty swallowing, speaking or breathing. Seek emergency care first, then call us.
  • A sudden severe headache unlike your usual one, or headache with fever and a stiff neck, confusion, or weakness or numbness beyond the treated area. Seek emergency care.
  • Any vision change, or a drooping eyelid.
  • Unusual neck weakness, or trouble holding your head up after Botox for migraine.
  • Signs of infection at an injection site: spreading redness, warmth, swelling, pus or fever.
  • Dizziness or faintness that does not pass after resting.
