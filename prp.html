PRP injections

One material, four different uses. Each has its own evidence, its own protocol and its own honest account of what it changes.

Performed by Dr. Shankar Mundluru, MD MBA, a board-certified physician

Platelets carry the growth factors the body uses to repair tissue. A small blood draw is spun in a centrifuge to separate and concentrate them, and that concentrate is placed where repair is wanted. The material is autologous, meaning it came from you, so there is nothing to be allergic to and nothing foreign left behind.

What PRP does depends entirely on where it goes. The evidence behind it in the scalp is not the evidence behind it under the eye, and treating them as one treatment hides the differences that matter. Each use has its own page.

Why anti-inflammatories undo it

PRP works by starting a controlled inflammatory response. Anti-inflammatories suppress exactly that response. Ibuprofen, naproxen, fish oil, vitamin E and flaxseed oil stop three days before treatment and stay stopped for three days afterwards. This is the most important instruction on this page, because it changes how well the treatment works rather than how comfortable it is. Prescribed blood thinners are a different matter and are never stopped without speaking to the physician who prescribed them.

What is true of all of them

The material is autologous. It is drawn from you at the start of the visit, concentrated, and used the same session, so there is nothing to be allergic to and nothing foreign left behind.

The result arrives slowly. PRP works by prompting your own repair response, so change follows the pace of tissue rather than the pace of an injection. Four to six weeks for the first sign, three to six months after a completed series for the fuller picture, across every indication.

Preparation is not incidental. How the plasma is separated, whether it is activated and what volume is delivered all change the result, and these details are not consistently reported in the published literature. It is the main reason results from different clinics are difficult to compare, and it is worth asking about anywhere you are treated.

Common questions

Is there anything in it besides my own blood?

No. The only thing injected is your own concentrated platelets, separated from a small draw taken the same visit. Nothing synthetic is added and nothing foreign is left behind, which is why allergy is not a consideration the way it is with materials that come from outside the body.

Why can I not take ibuprofen around PRP?

Because PRP works through a controlled inflammatory response, and anti-inflammatories suppress it. Ibuprofen, naproxen, fish oil, vitamin E and flaxseed oil should stop three days before treatment and stay stopped for three days afterwards. This is the single most important instruction for any PRP treatment, because it changes how well the treatment works rather than just how comfortable it is. Prescribed blood thinners such as apixaban, rivaroxaban, warfarin, clopidogrel or aspirin taken for cardiac reasons are different and should never be stopped without speaking to the prescribing physician.

Which of the four has the best evidence?

Hair restoration has the largest body of randomized evidence, with 43 trials and 1,877 participants pooled in a 2025 review. Microneedling with PRP for acne scarring has the most conclusive, with a 2026 meta-analysis reaching the threshold where a result is treated as settled rather than provisional. Facial skin quality is supported by a smaller set of trials, and the under-eye area is the newest of the four. Each page says where its own use stands.

Does it hurt?

The blood draw feels like any routine draw. Numbing cream goes on while the plasma is being prepared, so the injections themselves are pressure and brief stinging rather than sharp pain. The scalp and the under-eye area are the more sensitive of the treatment sites. Most people describe it as uncomfortable rather than painful, and it is over in minutes.

Who should not have PRP?

PRP is not performed in anyone with an active cancer or a history of blood cancer, in anyone with a platelet or clotting disorder, or where there is active infection, rash or an open wound in the treatment area. Pregnancy and breastfeeding are also reasons to wait. Because the treatment depends on your own platelets, anything that meaningfully affects them affects the result, which is part of what gets reviewed at consultation.

How much time should I allow?

Between 45 and 75 minutes in the clinic depending on the area, because the plasma has to be drawn and spun before anything is done. Downtime afterwards is short but not nothing: swelling for a day or two, and bruising that is more likely under the eye than elsewhere. Microneedling with PRP leaves redness like moderate sunburn for 24 to 48 hours, so that one is worth planning a clear day around.
