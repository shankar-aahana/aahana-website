SkinVive

An injectable that treats the quality of the skin rather than its shape. FDA approved for smoothness of the cheeks, and since June 2026 for lines on the neck.

SkinVive by Juvederm is a hyaluronic acid gel placed in the skin itself as a series of small microdroplets, rather than underneath it in a single depot. That difference in depth is the whole point of the product. It is not there to add shape or projection, and it does not behave like the fillers used for the cheekbone, chin or lips.

What it changes is the surface: smoothness, hydration and the way light falls on the skin. It contains lidocaine, so the injections are more comfortable than the number of them would suggest.

01

What it is approved for

SkinVive holds two FDA approvals, both in adults over 21. The first, granted in May 2023, is for improving skin smoothness of the cheeks. The second, granted in June 2026, is for wrinkles of the neck, and it is the first hyaluronic acid injectable approved for that area. Both are on-label uses.

What the trials found

The cheek approval rests on a randomized multicenter trial of 125 patients. Fifty-eight percent reached at least a one-point improvement on the cheek smoothness scale at one month, and fifty-six percent still had it at six months. Patients were also asked directly about their own skin. At six months, 72 percent were satisfied with its hydration against 24 percent before treatment, 63 percent with its radiance against 11 percent, and 83 percent described their skin as looking healthy against 38 percent at the start.

The neck approval rests on a separate trial of 105 patients. Just under 75 percent reached at least a one-point improvement at one month, and more than 65 percent still had it at six months. Reported side effects were redness, bruising, tenderness and swelling, all mild, and all resolving within two weeks without treatment.

The cheek trial enrolled across Fitzpatrick skin types I to VI, which is not something every aesthetic injectable can say about its own evidence.

What it does not do

It does not add volume. It will not lift a cheek, sharpen a jaw, project a chin or change the shape of anything, and if that is what you want it is the wrong product. It does not treat deep folds, it does not tighten loose skin, and on the neck it improves the lines in the skin rather than the laxity beneath them. Those are different problems with different answers, and sorting out which one you actually have is what the consultation is for.

How the result arrives, and how long it lasts

Some change is visible within a week or two, once the initial swelling and the small bumps at the injection points have settled. The trials measured their main result at one month and followed patients to six, which is also the interval at which retreatment was studied. Plan on a repeat visit around the six month mark rather than expecting a single treatment to hold indefinitely.

Common questions

Is this a filler?

It is hyaluronic acid, like most fillers, but it is used for a different purpose and placed at a different depth. Fillers sit under the skin and change its shape. SkinVive sits in the skin and changes its quality. Nothing about it adds projection or contour, so it is not an alternative to cheek or chin filler and it is not meant to be compared to one.

How does it compare to PRP for skin quality?

They aim at the same complaint from opposite directions. SkinVive is an FDA-approved manufactured product with randomized trial data behind it and a result that arrives within weeks. PRP uses your own blood, involves nothing manufactured, works by prompting your own repair response, and takes months. Neither is the better treatment in the abstract. Which fits depends on what you are treating, how quickly you want to see something, and how you feel about an injected product versus your own plasma.

Can it be used under the eye?

The approvals are for the cheeks and for the neck, and those are the areas treated here. The under-eye is thin, unforgiving skin that behaves differently from everything around it, and it has its own approach on the under-eye page.

Does the neck approval cover tech neck?

The June 2026 approval is for wrinkles of the neck, which includes the horizontal lines people mean by that phrase. What it treats is the line in the skin. It does not tighten a neck that has lost support underneath, and it is worth being clear at consultation about which of those is bothering you, because they look similar in a mirror and are not the same problem.

What are the small bumps afterwards?

The product is delivered as microdroplets, so immediately afterwards you can see and feel where each one went. They settle over a day or two as the gel disperses and the swelling around it resolves. Leave them alone rather than massaging them, and plan the appointment so you are not somewhere that matters that evening.

Who should not have it?

It is not used where there is active infection, inflammation or an unhealed wound in the treatment area, in anyone with a history of significant allergic reaction, or in anyone with a known lidocaine allergy, since the gel contains it. It is not given during pregnancy or breastfeeding. A history of keloid or hypertrophic scarring, autoimmune or connective tissue disease, and any previous injectable that did not go as expected all need to be raised beforehand rather than discovered afterwards.

How to prepare

Seven days before

  • Stop over-the-counter anti-inflammatories. Ibuprofen, naproxen, fish oil, vitamin E and flaxseed oil all increase bruising.
  • Stop alcohol. It dilates blood vessels and increases both bruising and swelling.
  • Do not stop prescribed blood thinners or anticoagulants such as aspirin taken for cardiac reasons, apixaban, rivaroxaban, warfarin or clopidogrel. Speak to the doctor who prescribed them. Contact us and we will plan around them.

Two weeks before

  • No dental work. Dental procedures can introduce bacteria into the bloodstream and raise infection risk. Schedule dental work well before or well after.
  • No waxing, threading or laser hair removal on the area.
  • No chemical peels, microneedling or resurfacing on the area.

Tell us before your appointment

  • Any allergy to lidocaine. The gel contains it.
  • Your previous injectable history: what product, where, when, and anything that did not go as expected.
  • Any autoimmune or connective tissue condition, and any history of keloid or hypertrophic scarring.
  • If you are pregnant or breastfeeding. This is not given during either.

The day itself

  • Arrive with a clean face and neck, no makeup on the area.
  • Eat something beforehand.
  • Allow time for numbing before the injections start.

The first 24 hours

  • Small raised points at each injection site are expected. They settle over a day or two as the gel disperses.
  • Redness, mild swelling and tenderness are normal. Bruising can appear and usually fades within a week.
  • Do not rub, press or massage the area.
  • Ice gently if it helps, wrapped in a cloth, never directly on the skin.
  • Avoid heat: hot showers, saunas, steam rooms, hot yoga.
  • No alcohol, no strenuous exercise, no makeup on the area.
  • For the neck, avoid anything tight against it, including high collars and scarves.

Days 2 to 14

Days 2 to 7

  • The injection points flatten and any bruising fades, sometimes darkening before it does.
  • Makeup is fine once the skin is unbroken, usually after the first day.
  • Gentle skincare only. No retinoids, acids or other actives on the area for 48 hours.
  • No facials, peels, microneedling or laser on the area for two weeks.
  • Sunscreen daily. Recently treated skin marks more easily.

Day 14 onwards

  • Actives can resume from day seven.
  • Change in smoothness and hydration is usually visible by now and continues to settle.
  • The result is assessed at one month, not in the first fortnight.

When to contact us straight away

  • Skin that looks white, pale or mottled anywhere near the treated area. This can indicate a blocked blood vessel and needs immediate attention. Call us or go to the nearest emergency room.
  • Any change in vision after treatment of the face. Seek emergency care immediately.
  • Swelling that worsens after 48 hours rather than improving.
  • Pain that increases after the first 24 hours.
  • Signs of infection: spreading redness, warmth, pus or fever.
  • Any lump or nodule appearing after day 14.
