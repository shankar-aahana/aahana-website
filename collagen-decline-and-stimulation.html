Collagen: what it is, when it starts declining, and what actually stimulates it

Most collagen advice is aimed at one kind of aging, on one kind of skin. Here is what the evidence supports, and how the answer changes depending on your age and your ancestry.

By Dr. Shankar Mundluru, MD MBA
Board-certified physician · Founder, Aahana Medical Aesthetics
September 4, 2026 · San Francisco

The short version

  • Collagen is the scaffolding of the dermis. Losing it shows up as laxity and thinning before it shows up as lines.
  • Decline is gradual through adulthood, then steep at menopause: up to 30 percent of dermal collagen in the first five years.
  • Ultraviolet exposure does more damage than time does, which makes photoprotection the highest-yield intervention available.
  • Skin ages differently across ethnicities, and in darker skin it usually shows as pigment and laxity rather than wrinkles.
  • Topical collagen does not become your collagen. What works is stimulating your own.

Collagen is the most talked about protein in aesthetics, and the most poorly explained. It is sold in powders, creams and drinks, almost always on the same premise: you are losing collagen, so put more collagen in.

That premise is wrong in a way that matters. You cannot replace collagen by eating it or applying it. You can only get your body to make more of its own, and what actually does that is well established and mostly unglamorous.

What collagen actually does

Collagen is the structural protein of the dermis, the living layer beneath the surface. Types I and III make up most of it in skin, woven with two partners:

Scaffolding fails differently from paint. As collagen density drops, skin loses firmness and thickness before it lines:

  1. FirstA subtle loss of support.
  2. ThenA face that looks more tired by evening; makeup sits differently.
  3. LateWrinkles: late evidence of something structural that began years earlier.

When it starts declining

Two processes run at once, and then a third arrives.

IntrinsicSlowly, from your twenties

Genetically programmed decline in production, regardless of behavior. Quiet, for decades.

ExtrinsicFast, mostly UV

UV degrades existing collagen and suppresses new synthesis. Compare your inner arm with your forearm: same person, same age, nothing alike. The most honest photoaging demonstration there is.

MenopauseIn a block

Estrogen drives collagen synthesis, elasticity and water retention. When it falls, dermal collagen drops by an estimated up to 30 percent within the first five years.1 Patients describe sudden change, and they are describing something real.

Time takes collagen slowly. Sunlight takes it fast. Menopause takes it in a block.

Aging does not look the same on everyone

Most anti-aging advice is built around wrinkles. For many of the people I will treat, wrinkles are not the main event.

Asian and Black skin have a thicker, more compact dermis, roughly in proportion to pigment. Black skin has more, larger and more nucleated fibroblasts, smaller collagen bundles and more macrophages:2 more scaffolding, better kept, for longer. So aging shows up differently. Choose a group to see how.2

Tone before texture

Wrinkles start about a decade later than in Caucasian skin, but pigmented spots come earlier and more often.

Descent, not etching

Sagging malar fat pads, soft tissue laxity, jowls and deeper nasolabial folds, centered on the midface and around the eyes rather than the brow.

Lines arrive early

Fine lines around the mouth and eyes come sooner, with more pronounced aging in the upper face.

Uneven tone leads

Mottled pigment, solar lentigines, seborrheic keratoses and rough texture are the main visible signs.

What actually stimulates collagen

Ranked by strength of evidence, not by margin.

  1. Strongest · preventionPhotoprotection

    In a trial of 903 adults over four and a half years, daily broad-spectrum sunscreen meant 24 percent less photoaging than occasional use.3 Nothing else here has that ratio of benefit to burden.

  2. Decades of controlled dataTopical retinoids

    The best-evidenced topical for collagen, fine lines and texture.1 Prescription tretinoin is stronger than retinol; both take months. In darker skin they also help pigment, but start gradually: irritation can leave marks of its own.

  3. Supporting layerAntioxidants

    Vitamins C, B3 and E support collagen, topical vitamin C most of all.1 Useful on top, not as a foundation.

  4. In clinicCollagen induction

    Controlled injury, heat or micro-wounds, prompts months of new collagen. Radiofrequency works on every skin tone, microneedling does the same mechanically, and fractional ablative resurfacing is more aggressive and needs more caution in deeper skin.1

What to do at home

The at-home half is not the consolation prize. Measured over years it does more than anything performed in a clinic.

  1. Daily broad-spectrum sunscreenThe highest-yield habit there is, with trial evidence.3 Deeper skin or a tendency to pigment: tinted, with iron oxides, which also blocks visible light.
  2. A retinoid, slowlyTwo or three nights a week, buffered with moisturizer, building as tolerated. Irritation is not a sign it is working.
  3. Vitamin C, morningsA cofactor in collagen synthesis and a reasonable antioxidant layer under sunscreen.
  4. No smoking, protected sleep, enough proteinBoth habits affect dermal repair, and synthesis needs raw material, underappreciated in people eating less than they think.

What does not work, or works less than advertised

Collagen creamA decent moisturizer

The molecule is far too large to cross the epidermis intact, and would not assemble into dermal fibers if it did. That is the whole of it.

Oral collagen peptidesInteresting, not settled

Several trials report modest gains in elasticity and hydration, but many are industry funded, endpoints vary, and effects are small next to sunscreen and retinoids. Take them if you like; do not substitute them for those two.

"Results in a fortnight"Swelling, not synthesis

New collagen is laid down over months, which is why honest before-and-after intervals are three and six months, not three weeks.

A word on expectations

The realistic goal is not to reverse decades. It is to slow the rate of loss, stimulate what can still be stimulated, and support the structure you have. Started in your thirties, that is largely a preventive project. Started in your fifties, it is more restorative and takes longer. Neither is too late, and neither is instant.

Common questions

At what age does collagen start to decline?

Intrinsic collagen decline begins in the twenties and proceeds gradually for decades. The steeper change comes at menopause, when falling estrogen is associated with a loss of up to 30 percent of dermal collagen within the first five years. Ultraviolet exposure accelerates loss at any age, which is why sun-exposed skin looks older than protected skin on the same person.

What actually stimulates collagen production?

In order of evidence: daily broad-spectrum sunscreen, which prevents loss rather than reversing it and reduced photoaging by 24 percent over four and a half years in a randomized trial; topical retinoids, the best-evidenced topical for collagen synthesis; antioxidants such as vitamin C as a supporting layer; and in-clinic collagen induction through radiofrequency, microneedling or fractional resurfacing, all of which provoke new collagen through controlled injury.

Do collagen creams work?

Not for adding collagen. The collagen molecule is too large to penetrate the epidermis intact, and it would not assemble into dermal fibers if it did. Products containing collagen generally act as moisturizers. Stimulating your own collagen with retinoids, sun protection and in-clinic treatment is what changes dermal collagen content.

Do collagen supplements work?

The evidence is mixed and weaker than for retinoids or sun protection. Several trials report modest improvements in skin elasticity and hydration, but many are industry funded and effect sizes are small. They are a reasonable addition for someone already doing the established things, and a poor substitute for them.

Does skin age differently in darker skin tones?

Yes. Asian and Black skin have a thicker, more compact dermis, and Black skin shows more numerous and larger fibroblasts with smaller collagen bundles. Wrinkle onset in East Asian skin is delayed by roughly a decade compared with Caucasian skin, though pigmented spots appear earlier. In Black and Hispanic skin, aging more often presents as midface volume loss, laxity and jowling rather than fine lines, and dyspigmentation is frequently the dominant visible marker.

Does menopause affect skin collagen?

Substantially. Estrogen stimulates collagen synthesis and supports dermal elasticity and water retention, and its decline at menopause is associated with a loss of up to 30 percent of dermal collagen in the first five years. This is why many women describe skin changing abruptly rather than gradually during that window.
