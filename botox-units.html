How many units, by area

Every area takes a different amount, and the range for each is wide. Here is what each part of the face takes, what treating it actually does, and what moves an individual number within the range.

Written by Dr. Shankar Mundluru, MD MBA
Board-certified physician · Founder, Aahana Medical Aesthetics
San Francisco · Dogpatch
Reviewed October 2026

Every figure below is in Botox-equivalent units. Dysport is measured on its own scale, where roughly two and a half to three units do the work of one Botox unit, so putting both on one scale is the only way to compare quotes. These are the ranges in standard practice. They describe which areas can be treated and what treating each one does, not a protocol.

Select an area to highlight it. Every area is listed in full alongside.

Forehead lines

Frontalis

10 to 20 units

Softens the horizontal lines across the brow. The frontalis is the only muscle that lifts the brow, which is why it is not treated on its own: relax it while the muscles pulling the brow down keep working and the brow sits lower than it started. The heavy, hooded look people describe after treatment is almost always a forehead treated in isolation rather than too much product.

Glabella

Corrugator, procerus

10 to 25 units

The vertical lines between the brows. Spread across the two corrugators and the procerus, following where the muscle actually sits, which varies between faces more than people expect. Strong frowners and heavier muscles sit at the top of the range.

Crow's feet

Orbicularis oculi

6 to 12 units per side

Lines fanning from the outer corner of the eye. How far they run onto the cheek, and whether they show at rest or only on smiling, changes both the amount and how far down the treatment extends.

Brow lift

Lateral orbicularis

2 to 6 units

A small amount at the tail of the brow releases the muscle pulling it down, letting the forehead lift it unopposed. The smallest amounts used on the face, and the area where exactly where it goes matters far more than how much.

Bunny lines

Nasalis

2 to 5 units per side

Diagonal lines across the bridge of the nose. They often become more obvious once the glabella is treated, because the nose takes over work the frown muscles no longer do.

Gummy smile

Levator labii superioris alaeque nasi

2 to 4 units per side

For a smile that shows a band of gum above the upper teeth. A small amount into the muscle beside the nose stops it pulling the lip quite so high, so the same smile shows less gum. Among the smallest amounts used on the face and among the least forgiving: too much, or placed too far out, lengthens the upper lip and flattens the smile on that side.

Masseter and jaw

Masseter

20 to 40 units per side

Treated for clenching and grinding, and to narrow a square jaw. The largest amount used anywhere on the face. Slimming comes from the muscle losing bulk over months rather than from the injection itself, so the change is gradual and needs repeating to hold.

Upper lip

Orbicularis oris

2 to 6 units

Softens fine vertical lines and rolls a little more lip outward. The least forgiving area on the face: a small amount past what is needed changes speech, drinking through a straw and the shape of a smile.

Lower lip

Orbicularis oris

2 to 4 units

The same vertical lines along the lower lip border. Smaller amounts than the upper lip, because the lower lip does more of the work in speech and the margin for getting it wrong is narrower.

Corners of the mouth

Depressor anguli oris

2 to 5 units per side

Releases the muscle that pulls the corner of the mouth down, which lifts a mouth resting in a downturn. Placement stays low and lateral, since the muscles that move the lower lip sit immediately alongside.

Chin

Mentalis

4 to 8 units

Smooths the dimpled, orange-peel texture that appears when the chin tightens. Usually treated alongside the corners of the mouth, since the two work together.

Jawline

Platysma, along the mandible

15 to 25 units

The platysma runs up from the chest and attaches along the jaw, where it pulls the jawline downward. Relaxing it there lets the jaw read sharper without touching the jaw itself. Usually treated together with the neck, since it is one continuous sheet.

Neck bands

Platysma

20 to 40 units

The vertical cords that stand out down the front of the neck when it tenses. The amount depends on how many bands there are and how prominent each one is, which is why the range is wide.

Why these are ranges

Because people are not the same. Muscle mass and muscle strength determine how much a muscle needs, and both vary widely. Men generally require meaningfully more than women for a comparable result, and an amount taken from an average under-treats, which gets mistaken for the product not working.

How you responded last time is the most useful information there is, which is why a second session is almost always more accurate than a first. The first session establishes how your muscles behave. Everything after that is adjustment.

More is not better past the point the muscle needs it. Beyond that, additional units buy cost and the flat, unmoving look people are trying to avoid, not extra duration.

Two areas that are not on the face

Chronic migraine

Head, neck and shoulders

155 units, repeated every 12 weeks.

A therapeutic protocol rather than a cosmetic one, covering the forehead, temples, back of the head, neck and shoulders. The amount is several times any cosmetic treatment, which surprises people who have only ever compared facial figures.

Excess sweating

Underarm

50 units per underarm.

Injected into the skin rather than the muscle, spread evenly across the sweating area. Palms and soles are treated as well, with a different technique.

Comparing one quote to another

Dysport is measured on its own scale. Roughly two and a half to three Dysport units do the work of one Botox unit, so a Dysport quote for the same area is always a larger number. A price per unit that looks lower often is not once both are on the same scale. Every figure on this page is already in Botox-equivalent units, so dividing a Dysport unit count by about three puts it alongside them.

Which product gets used here is decided at consultation. The treatment page covers how the two differ in onset and spread, and why one suits some areas better than the other.

Common questions

How many units do I need for my forehead?

Usually 10 to 20, and not on its own. The frontalis is the only muscle that lifts the brow, so relaxing it while the muscles pulling the brow down keep working is what leaves a brow sitting heavy. The glabella is treated at the same time, which adds its own amount on top.

Can I just have a few units to try it?

In some areas, yes. Brow position and the upper lip are genuinely small-dose areas. In the glabella and the masseter, too little produces a partial result that wears off early and reads as the treatment not working, which costs more than treating it properly once.

Does a bigger dose last longer?

Only where the first amount was too small for the muscle. Once a muscle has what it needs, more units do not extend duration. They add cost and raise the chance of an unnatural result.

How do I compare a Botox price with a Dysport price?

Put both on one scale first. Two and a half to three Dysport units are equivalent to one Botox unit, so divide a Dysport unit count by about three before comparing. Separately, an area price and a unit price are not the same thing: an area price assumes an amount, a unit price does not, and neither tells you what you are getting until you know both the number and the product.
