Your first visit

Forty five minutes. Imaging, examination, and a plan you leave holding.

Written by Dr. Shankar Mundluru, MD MBA
Founder, Aahana Medical Aesthetics
Reviewed September 2026

Most aesthetic consultations are a conversation about a menu. You arrive knowing roughly what you want, someone describes what it costs, and you decide. That works if the thing you came in for is the thing that would actually help.

This visit runs the other way around. The assessment comes first, and the plan comes out of it. The examination and the plan are Dr. Mundluru's, and neither is delegated.

Before you arrive

You will get an intake form. It asks about medical history, medications and supplements, prior procedures, and the products currently on your face. It also asks what actually bothers you, in your words, which is more useful than a treatment name. The prakriti questions are part of that form, so they are done before you arrive rather than taking up the appointment.

Come with a clean face. Visia imaging reads pigment, vascularity and porphyrins beneath the surface, and foundation, tinted sunscreen and concealer all interfere with that. If it is easier to arrive as you are, there is somewhere to take it off before imaging.

The prakriti questions

Part of the intake form, not a separate conversation on the day. A structured set of questions about sleep, digestion, temperature, stress, energy through the day, and how your skin behaves across seasons. In Ayurvedic terms this is constitutional assessment. In practical terms it is the part of the history a purely procedural consultation skips, and it is often where the pattern in someone's skin becomes legible.

Your answers are read before you come in, so the time in the room goes to examining you rather than to filling in a questionnaire. This is used as a framework for understanding tendencies, not as a diagnosis and not as a substitute for medical evaluation.

Visia imaging

Standardized photography under controlled lighting, in visible light, cross polarized light and ultraviolet. It separates what is on the surface from what sits below it: pigment in the dermis, vascular patterning, ultraviolet damage that has not surfaced yet, and the bacteria associated with acne.

The reason it comes first is that it is the only part of the visit that is not an opinion. It is a measurement, taken the same way each time, which means the next one can be compared to it.

Examination

This part is Dr. Mundluru, in person, under magnification. The imaging shows what is there. The examination establishes what it is: texture, scarring, laxity, whether pigment is epidermal or dermal, whether redness is vascular or inflammatory, and how your skin is likely to respond to energy or to injury.

Skin tone matters here more than most consultations admit. The risk profile of a laser, a peel or a needle depth is different in richly pigmented skin, and the assessment is done with that in front of it rather than as an afterthought.

Reading it together

The Visia results go on a screen and get explained. Not a score and a recommendation, but what each layer shows and what follows from it. Most people have never seen their own skin measured, and it tends to change what they thought the problem was.

02

What you leave with

A concrete plan. What to do first, what to do after that, what is worth doing and what is not, and what the sequence depends on. Where a treatment is the answer, the plan says which one and why that one. Where the answer is a change to your routine, or time, or something that is not a procedure at all, the plan says that instead.

It is written down. You take it with you whether or not you book anything.

Testing beyond imaging

Genomic testing, skin microbiome sequencing and laboratory markers are available, and they are not part of the standard visit. They are offered when the assessment points at something they would actually answer, and they carry their own cost. If nothing in the examination suggests they would change the plan, they are not recommended.

The reasoning behind each of them is set out on the precision medicine page.

Treatment on the same day

Sometimes. If the assessment is straightforward, the treatment is appropriate for you, and there is time, it can be done in the same appointment. Longer treatments, and anything that needs numbing time or a blood draw, are booked separately so they are not rushed.

Nothing is done on the day without the assessment supporting it. Preparation requirements differ by treatment and are set out on each treatment page.

Fees

There is a consultation fee. It applies toward treatment if you go ahead. Fees are discussed directly rather than published here.

Common questions

Do I need to know what treatment I want before I book?

No. What is useful is being able to say what bothers you and roughly how long it has been that way. The assessment is what turns that into a specific answer.

Can I be treated the same day?

Sometimes. It depends on what the assessment finds, whether the treatment is appropriate for you, and whether there is time left in the appointment. Treatments that need numbing time, a blood draw, or a longer session are booked separately rather than squeezed into the consultation.

Do I have to do the genomic and microbiome testing?

No. Visia imaging and the prakriti questions are included. The additional testing is optional, carries its own cost, and is only recommended when something in the examination suggests it would change what the plan says.

Does the consultation fee go toward treatment?

Yes. If you proceed with treatment, the consultation fee applies toward it.

How should I prepare?

Come with no makeup on, because it interferes with the imaging. Bring a list of your medications and supplements, and either bring the skincare products you are currently using or photograph the labels. Mention any isotretinoin use, recent procedures, and any history of keloid scarring or cold sores when you fill in the intake form.

Who will I see?

Dr. Mundluru examines you and builds the plan, and neither is delegated. Imaging, photographs and the practical parts of the appointment are handled around that, so the whole forty five minutes is not spent face to face with him. Every clinical decision in the visit is his.
