SkinVive or filler

Same material, different depth, completely different job. Most faces have a case for both, and the mistake is usually choosing one where the answer was a plan.

Written by Dr. Shankar Mundluru, MD MBA
Board-certified physician · Founder, Aahana Medical Aesthetics
San Francisco · Dogpatch
Reviewed September 2026

Both are hyaluronic acid gels. That is where the similarity ends.

Filler is placed under the skin, in a depot, where it occupies space and pushes outward. It is a structural material. Its job is projection and contour: a cheekbone, a chin, a jawline, a temple that has hollowed.

SkinVive is placed in the skin, as a series of small microdroplets spread across an area. It does not occupy space in any way you would notice as shape. Its job is the surface: smoothness, hydration, the way light falls on the skin.

The gels are even formulated differently to suit those two jobs, because a product that holds a contour and a product that disperses through the dermis have opposite requirements.

The question that decides it

Look in a mirror in flat light and describe what bothers you without naming a treatment.

If the words are flat, hollow, heavy, tired around the structure, sagging, or lost definition, that is shape, and shape is filler territory. If the words are dull, crepey, dry, rough, lacklustre, or that skin quality is not what it was, that is surface, and SkinVive is one of the answers to it.

People often have both. That is normal and it does not mean booking both at once. Which to do first is a sequencing question, and the usual answer is structure before surface, since correcting shape changes how the skin sits.

01

The most common mistake

Asking for filler because the skin looks tired. Filler placed to fix a surface problem adds volume nobody wanted, and it is the single most common route to a face that looks treated. If the complaint is quality rather than shape, adding structural material is the wrong tool no matter how well it is placed.

What SkinVive has that filler does not

Trial evidence for exactly what it claims. The cheek approval rests on a randomized multicenter trial of 125 patients, with 58 percent reaching at least a one-point improvement on the cheek smoothness scale at one month and 56 percent still there at six. Patient reported satisfaction with hydration went from 24 percent before treatment to 72 percent at six months, and with radiance from 11 percent to 63 percent. The trial enrolled across Fitzpatrick skin types I to VI.

The neck approval, granted in June 2026, rests on a separate trial of 105 patients, with just under 75 percent reaching a one-point improvement at one month and more than 65 percent holding it at six. It is the first hyaluronic acid injectable approved for the neck, which is worth knowing if that is the area bothering you, because until three months ago there was no on-label option there at all.

What filler has that SkinVive does not

The ability to change anything about your structure, twenty years of clinical use, a wide range of products matched to different tissue depths and different areas, and the fact that it can be dissolved. That last point matters more than people expect. A result you dislike is not permanent, which is a genuine safety feature and not a marketing line.

02

What we recommend

Usually both, in an order. Almost everyone asking about either of these has a structural component and a surface component, and treating only one leaves the other exactly as it was. Structure goes first, because correcting shape changes how the skin sits over it, and you want to see that before deciding what the surface still needs. SkinVive follows, once the filler has settled at around two weeks.

They are not done in the same appointment, and that is deliberate rather than cautious. Filler is assessed at day 14 and SkinVive at one month, so combining them in one visit makes it impossible to tell which one produced what. Doing them in sequence costs you a few weeks and tells you what each was worth.

Where this leaves the under-eye

Neither of these is offered under the eye here. SkinVive is approved for the cheeks and the neck, and those are the areas treated. Under-eye filler is not advertised by this practice. What is offered for that area is PRP, and the reasoning behind that choice is on its own page.

Common questions

Will SkinVive make my face look fuller?

No, and if that is what you are after it is the wrong product. It is placed in the skin as microdroplets rather than under it as a depot, so it does not occupy space in a way that reads as volume. What changes is smoothness and the quality of the surface. People often describe the result as looking well rather than looking different, which is exactly what it is designed to do.

Can I have both?

Yes, and plenty of people should, because most faces have a structural component and a surface component. They are not usually done in the same appointment. Structure first is the normal sequence, because correcting shape changes how the skin sits over it and you want to see that before deciding what the surface needs.

Which one is better value?

They are not comparable on cost per year, because filler lasts 9 to 18 months and SkinVive was studied with retreatment at around six. The better question is whether the thing you want changed is shape or surface, because paying less for a treatment aimed at the wrong problem is not value. Pricing for both is discussed at consultation.

Is SkinVive safe in darker skin?

The pivotal trial enrolled across Fitzpatrick skin types I to VI, and the approval covers all of them. That is not something every aesthetic injectable can say about its own evidence base. As with any injection, the general concern in richly pigmented skin is post-inflammatory pigmentation at needle entry points, which is managed through technique and aftercare rather than by avoiding the treatment.

How does SkinVive compare with PRP for skin quality?

They aim at the same complaint from opposite directions. SkinVive is a manufactured product with FDA approval and randomized trial data, and the result arrives within weeks. PRP uses your own blood, involves nothing manufactured, works by prompting your own repair response, and takes months. Neither is better in the abstract. It depends on what you are treating, how quickly you want to see something, and how you feel about an injected product versus your own plasma.

What if I am not sure which one I need?

That is the ordinary situation and it is what the consultation is for. Describing what bothers you is more useful than naming a treatment, because the same complaint can come from shape, from surface, from pigment or from a combination, and those have different answers. Imaging helps here, since what it measures separates surface change from what sits underneath it.
