Aura 3D imaging

A 3D digital twin of your face and neck, captured in an instant and analysed before anything is recommended. It is where every plan here starts.

Almost every decision people make about their skin is made in front of a mirror, in whatever light they happen to be standing in, filtered through a lifetime of opinions about their own face. It is a poor instrument for a decision that costs money and takes months to judge.

Aura is a 3D imaging system made in Switzerland by Hexagon. Multiple cameras and lighting units capture your face and neck at once, and its software builds a photo-realistic 3D model, a digital twin, that can be turned, relit and measured.1 Same position, same light, every visit. That is what makes two appointments comparable, which a mirror and a memory never are.

Why three dimensions matter

A flat photograph records color. It does not record shape. Much of what changes with age, and much of what injectables and devices are meant to change, is shape: volume lost from the temples or cheeks, a jawline that softens, lift that comes or goes. A 3D model measures those differences between visits instead of leaving them to a side-by-side impression.1

Color still matters, and it matters most in deeper skin. Pigment and redness are analysed separately, and against a deeper skin tone the two can look nearly identical in the room while needing very different treatment. Seeing them apart narrows the question before anyone touches your skin.

What it changes about your plan

It shows what is actually present rather than what is most visible. The loudest thing on a face is rarely the thing with the most to gain from treatment.

It establishes a baseline, which is the only way the next visit means anything. Without one, judging whether a treatment worked means comparing today against a memory, and memory is heavily biased toward believing the money was well spent.

And it changes the consultation itself. You see your own face the way a clinician sees it, which turns a list of recommendations into a conversation about evidence you are both looking at.

What it does not do

It measures. It does not diagnose. Aura quantifies features and models the face; it does not identify skin cancer, and it is not a substitute for examination by a clinician.

Its skin scores also deserve some skepticism. We have not found published data on how they perform across different skin types, so in deeper skin the images and the 3D measurements carry more weight here than any single number.

03

Where it sits among the other measurements

Imaging describes what your skin and face have done so far. Microbiome sequencing describes what is living on your skin right now. Lab work describes what is driving it from the inside. Imaging is the one everybody gets; the other two are ordered only where they would change something.

Common questions

Does Aura diagnose anything?

No. It measures and models; it does not diagnose. It does not identify skin cancer and it is not a substitute for examination by a clinician. Anything that looks concerning on an image gets examined properly in the room, and anything concerning on your skin should be examined whether or not it showed up in a scan.

Does it work on darker skin?

The 3D model and its measurements of shape, volume and proportion do not depend on skin tone. The skin scores deserve more caution: we have not found published data on how they perform across different skin types, so in deeper skin the images and the 3D measurements carry more weight here than any single score.

How should I prepare for imaging?

Come with a clean face and, if you can, no makeup, so the analysis reads your skin rather than what is on it. There is no fasting, no numbing and no downtime.

What is the 3D simulation for?

Aura can simulate the effect of treatment on areas such as the temples, cheeks, under-eyes, chin and jawline. A simulation is an illustration, not a promise. Its use is agreeing on what you are aiming for before anything is placed.

How often is it repeated?

Usually at around six months, and after any course of treatment. The point of a baseline is the comparison that follows it, measured on the same model rather than remembered.

Do I get to see the images?

Yes, in the room, on a screen, during the consultation. That is most of the reason for doing it first. It turns the appointment from a list of recommendations into a conversation about something you are both looking at.
