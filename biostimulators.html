A hyaluronic acid filler occupies space from the moment it is placed. A biostimulator does not. It is a material the body responds to by producing new collagen around it, and the collagen is the result. That difference sets the entire timeline, the aftercare and the way the treatment should be judged.

Sculptra or Radiesse

Both are biostimulators. They are made of different materials and are used in different places.

  • Sculptra is poly-L-lactic acid. It is used on the face. It adds no meaningful volume of its own, and the entire result is collagen your body produces over the following months.
  • Radiesse is calcium hydroxylapatite. It is used for the hands and décolletage. It gives an immediate volume improvement and then stimulates collagen over the following three to four months.

Which one is used, and whether a biostimulator is the right approach at all, is decided at consultation after examination.

Why it cannot be judged at two weeks

Sculptra is injected as a diluted solution, and that solution causes real swelling on the day. People often leave looking fuller than they will look for the next several weeks. When the swelling resolves at two or three days, the face returns to close to where it started, because the product itself was never the volume.

This is the point most people are not told. Looking unchanged at day seven is not a failed treatment. Collagen begins building at around four weeks, and the result from a full series is read at six months.

Biostimulators also cannot be dissolved. Hyaluronic acid filler can be reversed with hyaluronidase; poly-L-lactic acid and calcium hydroxylapatite cannot. That makes placement and patient selection matter more, not less.

Common questions

How is Sculptra different from filler?

Filler adds volume the moment it is placed and can be dissolved. Sculptra adds almost no volume of its own. It is poly-L-lactic acid, and it prompts your own tissue to produce collagen over the following months. Results build gradually rather than appearing at once, last up to two years after a full series, and cannot be reversed. The two treatments answer different questions, and they are sometimes used together at different visits.

Why do I look the same two weeks after Sculptra?

Because that is how it works. The fullness on the day of treatment comes from the diluted solution the product is delivered in, not from the product. When that resolves over two or three days, you return to close to your starting point. Collagen building begins at around four weeks, most people notice change between four and eight weeks, and the full result from a three-session series is read at six months.

What is the 5-5-5 rule and does it matter?

After every Sculptra session you massage the treated area for five minutes, five times a day, for five days. It distributes the product evenly through the tissue and substantially reduces the risk of nodules forming. It is the single most important thing you do at home, and skipping it is the most common reason a result comes out uneven. The technique is demonstrated before you leave the clinic.

How many Sculptra sessions will I need?

The standard protocol is three sessions spaced four to six weeks apart, then an annual maintenance session to sustain the collagen that has been built. How much is used at each session depends on the degree of volume loss and on how you responded to the previous one.

Can biostimulators be dissolved if I do not like the result?

No. Hyaluronic acid filler can be reversed with hyaluronidase. Poly-L-lactic acid and calcium hydroxylapatite cannot be dissolved, and they resolve only as the body breaks them down over time. This is why conservative dosing, correct placement and a careful consultation matter more with biostimulators than with reversible products.

Are biostimulators useful for facial volume loss during GLP-1 treatment?

They are one of the options considered. Rapid weight loss removes facial fat and can leave skin without the structure it was resting on, and a treatment that rebuilds collagen addresses the structural side of that rather than only filling the hollow. Whether it is appropriate for you, and when in the course of weight loss it should be done, is decided at consultation.

What should I tell you before treatment?

Any history of keloid or hypertrophic scarring, since these treatments work by driving collagen production. Any previous filler or biostimulator, including what was used, when, and anything that did not go as expected. Any autoimmune or connective tissue condition. Any allergy, particularly to lidocaine. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, biostimulators are not performed.

Does Fitzpatrick skin type affect eligibility?

No. Biostimulators are injected below the surface and do not rely on light or heat, so skin type does not change eligibility or the protocol. Aftercare and injection technique are the same across all six types.

How to prepare

Seven days before

  • Stop over-the-counter anti-inflammatories. Ibuprofen, naproxen, fish oil, vitamin E and flaxseed oil all increase bruising.
  • Stop alcohol. It increases bruising and swelling.
  • Stop high-dose vitamin C above 1,000 mg a day.
  • Do not stop prescribed blood thinners or anticoagulants such as aspirin taken for cardiac reasons, apixaban, rivaroxaban, warfarin or clopidogrel. Do not stop any of these without speaking to the doctor who prescribed them. Contact us instead and we will plan around them.

Two weeks before

  • No dental procedures. Dental work can introduce bacteria and raises infection risk around injected material.
  • No aggressive treatments on the area, including peels, microneedling and laser.

Tell us before your appointment

  • Any history of keloid or hypertrophic scarring. These treatments work by driving collagen production, which can behave unpredictably in skin prone to abnormal scarring.
  • Any previous filler or biostimulator: what was used, when, and anything that did not go as expected.
  • Any autoimmune or connective tissue condition.
  • Any known allergy, particularly to lidocaine.
  • If you are pregnant or breastfeeding. Biostimulators are not performed during pregnancy.

The day itself

  • Arrive with the treatment area clean.
  • Eat something beforehand.
  • For Radiesse on the hands, remove rings and jewelry before you arrive.

The 5-5-5 massage

After every Sculptra session: five minutes, five times a day, for five days.

  • The massage distributes the product evenly through the tissue and substantially reduces the risk of nodules forming.
  • It is not optional, and it is the main thing that determines whether the result is even.
  • The technique is demonstrated before you leave the clinic.
  • Set five reminders on your phone on the day of treatment. People miss sessions on day three and four, not day one.
  • Massage only the areas that were treated with Sculptra. If anything else was done at the same visit, you will be told exactly which areas to leave alone.

This applies to Sculptra. Radiesse does not require it.

Sculptra, the first two weeks

The first 24 to 48 hours

  • Noticeable swelling and puffiness is normal. It comes from the solution the product is delivered in, not the product, and it settles within two to three days.
  • Ice gently, wrapped in a cloth.
  • Do not press or touch the area other than the prescribed massage.
  • Avoid heat: saunas, hot showers and steam rooms.
  • No strenuous exercise for 24 hours.

Days 3 to 14

  • As the swelling resolves you will look close to how you looked before treatment. This is expected. It is not a sign the treatment did not work.
  • Continue the massage through day five.
  • Gentle skincare is fine. Avoid active treatments on the area for two weeks.
  • Retinoids and other actives can resume after day seven. They support collagen production rather than interfering with it.

Radiesse, hands and décolletage

Hands

  • Swelling and bruising for three to five days is normal. The hands can look quite puffy immediately afterwards.
  • Keep hands elevated when resting for the first 24 hours.
  • Wear loose gloves or keep the hands protected during any activity.
  • No long baths, and no dishwashing without gloves, for 24 hours.
  • Avoid strenuous hand activity for 24 hours.
  • The volume improvement is visible straight away. The collagen builds over the following three to four months.

Décolletage

  • Mild redness and swelling for 24 to 48 hours is normal.
  • Avoid tight, abrasive or synthetic fabric over the area for 24 hours.
  • SPF 50 on the chest every day from here on. This area takes more UV than people expect, and sun exposure is what undoes the result.
  • The result builds over three to four months as collagen is produced.

Weeks 4 to 24

  • Collagen building starts at around four weeks. Most people notice change between four and eight weeks, in the mirror rather than in photographs.
  • The full result from a three-session Sculptra series is read at six months, not before.
  • Drink water consistently through the series. Sculptra performs better in well hydrated tissue.
  • Daily SPF 50. UV degrades collagen, which is the entire thing you are paying to build.
  • Sculptra sessions are spaced four to six weeks apart, three in the initial series, then annual maintenance.
  • Radiesse is repeated every 12 to 18 months.

When to contact us straight away

  • Skin that turns white, pale or mottled near the treated area. This can indicate a blocked vessel. Call the clinic immediately or go to the nearest emergency room.
  • Any change in vision after facial treatment. Seek emergency care immediately.
  • Swelling that is worsening after 48 hours rather than settling.
  • Pain that is increasing after the first 24 hours.
  • Signs of infection: spreading redness, warmth, pus or fever.
  • Firm lumps or nodules appearing after day 14, particularly if the massage was not completed.
